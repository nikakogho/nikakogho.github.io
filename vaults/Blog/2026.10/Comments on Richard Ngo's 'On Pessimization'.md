So his basic idea is that just as optimization means orienting your actions to get more of what you want (increase utility), pessimization is orienting your actions to decrease your utility, which he claims is what happened to all these AGI labs that started as "I must do it safer and faster than others".

It seems the whole point of writing this up his "hey guys, slow down and reflect on what the hell you are doing, stop racing to the bottom out of fear". And, ah, this perhaps applies to me as well. But I mean, his way of offering an alternative is "well maybe it takes 20 years from RSI to AGI so maybe we have enough time to actually really solve alignment with ambitious mech interp or with agent foundations" and like, I'm not convinced by that bro, u don't actually have a better plan than "contribute to prosaic alignment so at least RSI begins with models that are more likely to be on our side". Well maybe more people moving into governance and advocating for slowdown / more technical research becoming "hey public and everybody, here is why these alignment techniques don't work and here are documented safety incidents". idk man. I guess governance makes sense but I have 2 issues:
1) surely alignment still has to be solved soon unless your plan is to delay ASI by like centuries, which would require a very totalitarian world and would also be very sad (unless we solve uploading fast? ah s-risks of that)
2) moving into governance feels way too far outside my comfort zone, and moving into technical work whose purpose is to show these alignment methods don't work "so go pause" feels, ah, defeatist and meh, but I may eventually change my mind on this. ah fuck, "update all the way" is not easy at all.

I like his closing line, explaining why he now leans to virtue ethics: "one way of understanding virtues is as traits that guard us against sliding into pessimization, thereby allowing us to more robustly steer the world towards desired outcomes"
