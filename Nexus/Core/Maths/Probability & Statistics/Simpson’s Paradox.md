A paradox in [[Statistics]] where a trend is true for many groups individually but opposite seems to be the case collectively, sometimes called BBG (bad-bad-good). Example:
![simpsons_paradox.jpeg](simpsons_paradox.jpeg)

Problem is the group marker (like age) might be a confounder that affects both X and Y that we are measuring, which would be why controlling for our group marker could give opposite result from aggregate data’s prediction. We must consider the rules of [[Causality|causality]] to decide whether to control the group marker or not for the result.
